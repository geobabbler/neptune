## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. Agents do the analysis, humans own the framing**

Geospatial FM's latest episode has Dekart founder Volodymyr Bilonenko walking through geoSQL, a Claude skill that checks images of its own maps while building a Dekart output. David G. Smith summarizes an experiment where coding agents refused to p-hack until researchers reframed the ask as "responsible uncertainty quantification."

*Why this matters:* The agentic GIS thread is now about workflow, not whether it works. Bill Dollins keeps arguing that AI still needs you to understand your business. Smith's example shows why. A well-phrased request can steer an agent anywhere, so the person writing the prompt owns the result.

**2. Official population data, two very different vintages**

ProximityOne digs into the Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 county estimates, where growth slowed in most of the 3,143 counties as net international migration fell. Revolutionary GIS posted a pointer to Cambodia's 2008 village census on the Open Development Cambodia portal.

*Why this matters:* Government is the best-narrated segment in these feeds, but usually through contracts. Here it's the data itself, with a marketing angle on one side and open access on the other. Commercial demographic use is rare in this ecosystem, and "usefulness over openness" is still a live debate.

**3. Swiss data exchange picks up modern tooling**

Spatialists flagged two INTERLIS items. Stefan Ziegler is prototyping IBX, a binary encoding of INTERLIS 2 data with selective access, and geowerkstatt shipped version 1.0 of its VS Code INTERLIS 2 extension. Both come through one curator, so this is a thinner thread than the others today.

*Why this matters:* INTERLIS is a model-first national standard, and IBX is a cloud-native bet on it. That's the same format-consolidation conversation as GeoParquet, COGs and Zarr, played out in a European setting the English-language feeds mostly miss.
