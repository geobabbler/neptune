**3. Climate volatility is pressure-testing how the industry predicts environmental risk**

ICEYE's blog pushed back on the assumption that a "lower risk" El Niño season means no risk at all, pointing to historical exceptions like Hurricane Danny's 1997 Alabama rainfall record during a supposedly quiet season. Meanwhile EarthStuff flagged new research using airborne gamma-ray spectrometry, normally a geophysics tool, to map hidden soil properties and predict where drought-driven tree die-off will hit hardest, developed after Western Australia's Jarrah forests lost 400 square kilometers to a single eighteen-month drought.

*Why this matters:* Two very different data sources, insurance-grade seasonal forecasting and geophysical remote sensing, are being pushed to answer the same question: where exactly will climate stress hit next. Neither offers certainty, but both show the industry building sharper tools for a problem that keeps getting bigger.
