## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. Utility and construction data gets a readiness check**

Safe Software's FME blog lays out a six-step, configuration-driven pattern for migrating to the Esri Utility Network, warning that data can load fine and still fail to validate or trace. Esri's ArcGIS Blog ran a piece on why asset management programs stall and another on deploying an enterprise geodatabase in SAP HANA. vGIS argues faster field-to-office data workflows protect construction margins that run 3 to 7 percent.

*Why this matters:* Utilities and construction are the commercial verticals these feeds rarely cover. Here they show up as plumbing problems, not AI promises. The unglamorous work of data readiness is where buyers actually spend money, and vendors are writing to them directly.

**2. Heat, flood and fire with places attached**

Earth Observation News recapped Cornell's Stephan Schmidt on how urban form drives heat islands, with work spanning Europe and thousands of African cities. Earth Imaging Journal covered record Gandak River flooding in Nepal and India. The FOSS4G NA team published a water, wildfire and resilience agenda built around evacuation maps and watershed work.

*Why this matters:* Hazard coverage keeps landing on named places with clear decision-makers, which is the demand-side texture the ecosystem usually lacks. Africa and South Asia also get real airtime here, a welcome counterweight to the North American and European skew.

**3. Standards and AI tooling settle into the mainstream**

MappingGIS explains GeoZarr, noting that OGC itself points to it as the preferred cloud-native format for multidimensional data. Esri published a guide to interpreting embeddings with AutoML and SHAP in ArcGIS Pro. The FOSS4G NA agenda also lists a talk on a spatial MCP server for giving LLMs a sense of place.

*Why this matters:* Zarr versus COG is the format consolidation fight to watch, and an OGC nod matters. Embeddings buzz has cooled since spring, but Esri treating them as ordinary analyst tooling is its own signal. MCP is now a conference-talk staple.
