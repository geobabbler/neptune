## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. Spatial data infrastructure shrinks to files and scripts**

Spatialists picked up David G. Smith's argument that Portolan signals a quiet reinvention of spatial data infrastructure, with cloud-native data sitting in object storage instead of behind heavy servers. Brian's Geek Blog posted updates to misc_gis_scripts, a small repo for pulling open data into PostgreSQL and PostGIS.

*Why this matters:* The cloud-native crowd keeps swapping servers for files, and hobbyist tooling like Brian's shows how low the bar has fallen. The open question is who maintains the catalogues once the heavy portals are gone.

**2. AI adoption is outrunning the people supposed to govern it**

David G. Smith cited a Dataiku survey where 84% of CIOs say employees build AI applications faster than IT can review them, and fewer than half of organizations have clear governance controls. IQGeo's 2026 Ground Truth Survey asks a similar question of telecom operators, looking at AI maturity and what stands between them and autonomous networks.

*Why this matters:* Most geo AI coverage is supply-side cheerleading. Surveys of buyers are rare, and telecom is exactly the kind of commercial vertical this ecosystem barely covers. Governance gaps are where agentic GIS ideas will actually stall.

**3. Orbit as a watch tower for infrastructure and sovereignty**

ICEYE published a piece on protecting undersea cables in Northern Europe with SAR satellites. GoGeomatics' international digest flagged Europe's sovereign EO service and Google putting AI chips in orbit with Planet, and TerraWatch ran its weekly Earth Observation briefing.

*Why this matters:* European sovereignty is moving from op-ed framing into procurement and use cases. Seabed cables are a concrete defense-adjacent customer for SAR, and ICEYE's post shows how commercial operators pitch themselves into that role.
