## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. Applied geospatial education fills a persistent gap**

Open Geospatial Solutions posted two videos in the window: one walking through mapping the 2026 Nepal floods with free high resolution satellite imagery, another continuing its Intro to GIS Programming series into Python variables and data types. Maps Mania took on the Coastline Paradox, Benoit Mandelbrot's 1967 puzzle about why Great Britain's coastline gets longer the more finely you measure it.

*Why this matters:* Practical tutorials, on satellite disaster mapping and on the math underneath every map, are exactly what the feeds chronically underserve. Three posts doing real teaching in one window is worth noting, especially with the actual Nepal floods giving the satellite imagery lesson immediate stakes rather than a hypothetical.

**2. Tooling keeps getting shaped by real practitioner needs**

Chris Holmes shipped geoparquet-io 1.4.0, folding in more than 60 pull requests since the 1.0 release and crediting most of the recent progress to real world dataset conversion work driven by Portolan's command line tool. VertiGIS published a head to head comparison of how ArcGIS Experience Builder and VertiGIS Studio handle report generation and printing, a capability web GIS builders need constantly and vendors rarely explain clearly.

*Why this matters:* Cloud native format tooling and web GIS platforms both got attention today not for flashy new features but for unglamorous production needs, printable reports and s3 compatible storage. That's the maturation phase the Cloud Native Geospatial Infrastructure crowd has been pushing toward all year, tooling built for what practitioners actually run into.

**3. The industry keeps talking to itself**

GoGeomatics previewed the finalists for Bentley Systems' 2026 Year in Infrastructure Going Digital Awards, its usual roundup of who made the shortlist across planning, design, construction and operations categories. TerraWatch Space put out its own weekly Earth Observation briefing. Neither post breaks new ground so much as it keeps the industry's self referential newsletter and awards machinery running on schedule.

*Why this matters:* A big share of the feed ecosystem's volume is the industry curating itself, awards shortlists, weekly digests, conference recaps. That's not nothing, it keeps practitioners oriented, but on a quiet posting day it's worth naming for what it is rather than treating it as news.

## Top Five Posts

**1. Announcing geoparquet-io 1.4.0!** — *9eo.org (Chris Holmes)*
Chris Holmes details what's shipped in geoparquet-io since the 1.0 release, more than 60 pull requests covering s3 compatible storage support and a fresh comparison of Hilbert and STR sorting for spatial indexing. It's a rare look at a widely used open format tool evolving through actual production use rather than a roadmap announcement.
→ [Read it](https://9eo.org/blog/announcing-geoparquet-io-1-4-0/)

**2. How Great is Great Britain?** — *Maps Mania*
Maps Mania revisits Benoit Mandelbrot's 1967 Coastline Paradox, the finding that a coastline's measured length keeps growing the finer your ruler gets, and traces why there's no single correct answer to how long Britain's coast actually is. It's sharp, accessible writing about a measurement problem that sits underneath every map a reader has ever used.
→ [Read it](http://googlemapsmania.blogspot.com/2026/08/how-great-is-great-britain.html)

**3. Mapping the 2026 Nepal Floods with Free High-Resolution Satellite Imagery** — *Open Geospatial Solutions*
Open Geospatial Solutions walks through mapping this year's Nepal floods using satellite imagery that costs nothing to access, a concrete demonstration of disaster response mapping rather than a vendor pitch. It's the kind of applied remote sensing walkthrough the feeds rarely produce outside of conference talks.
→ [Read it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UDO1BCwOAAc)

**4. Reporting and Printing in ArcGIS Experience Builder and VertiGIS Studio: A Comparison** — *VertiGIS*
VertiGIS lays out, feature by feature, how its Studio product and Esri's Experience Builder handle generating printable site plans and PDF reports from a web GIS app. Reporting and printing are unglamorous but constant practitioner needs, and this is a more specific comparison than the usual vendor capability list.
→ [Read it](https://www.vertigis.com/vertigis_blog/arcgis-experience-builder-vs-vertigis-studio-reporting-and-printing/)

**5. Anonymous project in Arles** — *Mappery*
Mappery covers an exhibition at Arles that reinserts photographer Omar Victor Diop into segregation era North American family photos, including one staged in front of the Unisphere globe at the 1964 World's Fair. It's an unusual entry for the feeds, cartographic imagery used as a tool for social commentary rather than data or navigation.
