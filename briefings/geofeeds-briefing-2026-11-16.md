## Highlights

**1. Social debt in the age of AI** — *Spatialists – geospatial news*
Spatialists points to Brianna Corremonte of Development Seed, who argues that AI and agentic coding are piling up social debt in open-source communities. Her examples are specific: newcomers vanish, maintainers field AI-generated pull requests, and lone developers ship more and more tools in isolation. It's a useful counterweight to the agentic GIS enthusiasm, since it asks what happens to the people who keep the open stack running.
→ [Social debt in the age of AI](https://spatialists.ch/posts/2026/11/15-social-debt-in-the-age-of-ai/)

**2. GeoAI Imagery Data Pipelines** — *Thoughts & Insights — David G. Smith*
Smith's starting point is that the hardest part of serious EO machine learning usually isn't the model, it's the data. The post looks at geoai-datacubes, a new open-source project that turns multi-mission inputs like Sentinel-2 optical, Sentinel-1 radar, elevation and land cover into something ML systems can actually consume. It's a pipeline story rather than another model announcement, which is rarer than it should be.
→ [GeoAI Imagery Data Pipelines](https://davidgsmith.net/thoughts/geoai-imagery-data-pipelines.html)

**3. GeoLibre vs./und RustGIS?** — *#geoObserver*
GeoLibre and RustGIS are two new open-source GIS projects that surfaced almost at the same time from the same developer team, and this German-language post asks the obvious question of why there are two. It doesn't answer it directly but points to a project comparison page and a GitHub discussion on where both go from here. Read it alongside the social debt piece, because it's a live case of small teams shipping parallel tools.
→ [GeoLibre vs./und RustGIS?](https://geoobserver.de/2026/11/16/geolibre-vs-und-rustgis/)

**4. 🌎 When Republicans Loved Nature** — *The Planet*
The author opens with a childhood encyclopedia in the Netherlands and a drawing of Half Dome in Yosemite pored over hundreds of times, and the realization years later of having learned about the world through American eyes. From there the piece looks back at an era of Republican support for conservation. It's a personal essay rather than technical content, but conservation and protected areas get little attention in these feeds.
→ [🌎 When Republicans Loved Nature](https://theplanet.substack.com/p/when-republicans-loved-nature)
