**1. The road to Portolan** — *Spatialists*
Chris Holmes tells the personal story behind Portolan, a serverless spatial data infrastructure meant to make cloud-native formats like GeoParquet, COG and Zarr the default way to share geodata. It's a rare first-person account from one of the people actually building the cloud-native stack, not just writing about it, and it frames AI as part of the bet on making that infrastructure accessible.
→ [Read it](https://spatialists.ch/posts/2026/09/13-the-road-to-portolan/)

**2. MVT Server: publica teselas vectoriales desde PostGIS con MapLibre** — *MappingGIS*
MappingGIS covers MVT Server, a new open-source Rust project that serves vector tiles directly out of PostGIS for use with MapLibre, cutting a chunk out of the usual tile-serving stack. It's concrete cloud-native tooling from a Spanish-language voice the mostly English feed ecosystem rarely surfaces.
→ [Read it](https://mappinggis.com/2026/09/mvt-server-publica-teselas-vectoriales-desde-postgis-con-maplibre/)

**3. The Evolution of Commercial Satellite Imagery in the U.S. Government** — *Project Geospatial*
Project Geospatial traces seven decades of the US commercial satellite imagery ecosystem, crediting the intelligence officers, researchers, risk-taking founders and procurement officials who built it rather than just the companies involved. It's a rare long-form historical treatment of how government demand actually shaped a commercial industry, sitting squarely in the sovereignty and defense conversation running through the feeds all year.
→ [Read it](https://projectgeospatial.org/geospatial-frontiers/the-evolution-of-commercial-satellite-imagery-in-the-us-government)

**4. Closing the loop: GeoAI from scan to decision** — *Spatial Source*
Spatial Source reports on CSDILA researchers building an AI process that runs real-world cityscape geospatial assignments end to end, from scan to decision, rather than stopping at a demo. It's a small but concrete data point in a GeoAI conversation that landscape context flags as heavy on announcements and light on deployed systems.
→ [Read it](https://www.spatialsource.com.au/closing-the-loop-geoai-from-scan-to-decision/)

**5. What's in a name? Naming things in OpenStreetMap** — *OpenStreetMap Blog*
The OpenStreetMap Blog digs into how the project's name= tag works, noting that volunteers have mapped 115 million names worldwide and walking through how local-language naming actually gets decided on the ground. It's a plain-language explainer on a piece of OSM's data model that shapes how the map reads in every country it covers.
→ [Read it](https://blog.openstreetmap.org/2026/09/13/whats-in-a-name-naming-things-in-openstreetmap/)

**6. GeoGuessr Gets an Antipodean Time Machine** — *Maps Mania*
Maps Mania rounds up two new GeoGuessr spinoffs, including TimePin, which asks players to guess not just where a photo was taken but when, using a time slider alongside the usual map pin. It's a lighter pick, but it's the kind of daily web-mapping curation the blog has built its reputation on.
