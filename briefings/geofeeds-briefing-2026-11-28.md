## Highlights

**1. Geospatial AI Has a Benchmark Problem. This Paper Finally Says the Quiet Part Out Loud.** — *Thoughts & Insights — David G. Smith*
Smith walks through a new arXiv paper, "geo-eval-harness: An Evaluation Harness for Geospatial Foundation Models," which argues that inconsistent evaluation may be hiding how well EO foundation models really perform. He leads with a prior review of 152 geospatial foundation model papers that found dozens of cases where the same model posted substantially different scores on the same benchmark. It's a useful corrective for anyone comparing state-of-the-art claims across land cover, flood mapping or building extraction.
→ [Geospatial AI Has a Benchmark Problem. This Paper Finally Says the Quiet Part Out Loud.](https://davidgsmith.net/thoughts/geospatial-ai-has-a-benchmark-problem-this-paper-finally-says-the-quiet-part-out-loud.html)

**2. Why Top of Bank Matters and How to Verify It With GIS and Lidar** — *Geography Realm*
The post uses GIS and lidar to check the top of bank line drawn on Midpen Open Space's plans for a horse stable in the Santa Cruz Mountains, and finds that it contradicts the actual topography. It's applied point cloud work with a real regulatory stake, a combination the feeds almost never carry.
→ [Why Top of Bank Matters and How to Verify It With GIS and Lidar](https://www.geographyrealm.com/top-of-bank-matters-gis/)

**3. Climate Change Intensifies Hydrological Seasonality In Denmark - Insights From An Integrated Model Assessment** — *EarthStuff*
EarthStuff shares a HESS paper that runs the National Hydrological Model of Denmark against an ensemble of 17 regional climate projections. The results point to wetter winters with higher streamflow and groundwater, but also deeper summer deficits in soil moisture as evapotranspiration rises. That seasonal split matters for anyone planning around water supply or agriculture.
→ [Climate Change Intensifies Hydrological Seasonality In Denmark - Insights From An Integrated Model Assessment](https://earthstuff.substack.com/p/climate-change-intensifies-hydrological)

**4. Intro to GIS Programming | Week 15: Cloud Computing with Earth Engine and Geemap** — *Open Geospatial Solutions*
The latest installment of a weekly GIS programming course covers cloud computing with Google Earth Engine and the geemap Python package. The feed carries only the title, so treat it as a pointer, but it's the kind of hands-on cloud tutorial working analysts keep asking for.
→ [Intro to GIS Programming | Week 15: Cloud Computing with Earth Engine and Geemap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSf0iYTwRZ4)

**5. Mapping Libraries in the Map Library: Collection Items Created by Library of Congress Staff** — *Worlds Revealed*
Reference librarian Evangeline Gahn highlights three maps that Geography and Map Division staff made for librarians and map collections in Washington, D.C. Her point is that these maps reflect their makers' own information needs, unlike the commercially published maps that fill most of the collection.
→ [Mapping Libraries in the Map Library: Collection Items Created by Library of Congress Staff](https://blogs.loc.gov/maps/2026/11/mapping-libraries-in-the-map-library-collection-items-created-by-library-of-congress-staff/)
