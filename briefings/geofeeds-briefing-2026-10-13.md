## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. Public money is steering the EO market**

Project Geospatial asked whether Washington's procurement system can keep pace with the commercial satellite industry it helped build. Earth Imaging Journal reported that ESA InCubed and Spain's space agency are co-funding eight wildfire projects with €3m combined. Spatial Source covered the first Sentinel-3C images, released two weeks after launch.

*Why this matters:* Government is still the anchor customer, and the story is shifting from who sells imagery to who buys it and how fast. Europe is funding small, targeted pilots while the US debates acquisition plumbing. Project Geospatial's framing needs corroboration, so watch for other voices.

**2. Open source is gathering in person**

The QGIS User Conference drew more than 420 people from over 30 countries to Laax, Switzerland, with about 90 talks and six keynotes, including Anita Graser on AI in the QGIS ecosystem. FOSS4G North America published a first-time attendee guide that points newcomers to approachable workshops and talks.

*Why this matters:* The community is putting real effort into onboarding and sustaining the people and infrastructure behind the tools. A Swiss MP opening a QGIS conference on open source also fits the European sovereignty thread, where open software is becoming policy.

**3. Office and field tools keep merging**

Blue Marble Geographics rolled separate Global Mapper products into a single three-tier subscription and framed it as groundwork for field execution. Topcon's INTERGEO recap stressed reality capture, GNSS and mobile mapping as connected workflows. Juniper Systems launched the Alta rugged Android tablet with on-device AI.

*Why this matters:* Surveying and field collection are commercial territory the feeds rarely cover well, and vendors are bundling hardware, software and edge AI into one pitch. Subscription consolidation also shifts how buyers pay, which matters more than any single feature.
