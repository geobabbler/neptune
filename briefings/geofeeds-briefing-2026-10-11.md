## Quiet Day Across the Feeds

Quiet day across the feeds. Here are the highlights.

**Infrastructure and craft: fewer servers, more tooling**

David G. Smith wrote about Portolan, an open-source project that treats spatial data as files in object storage with open metadata instead of a stack of servers and portals. Bill Dollins built a WordPress plug-in for narrative maps, inspired by the opengeos maplibre-gl-storymaps project but written fresh because WordPress plug-ins are a different beast.

*Why this matters:* The cloud-native community keeps pushing the work toward "just publish files." Small practical builds like Dollins' show the payoff, since web mapping frameworks still get little blog coverage and tools that fit an existing CMS lower the bar for everyone else.

**The role of the GIS professional keeps shifting**

EarthStuff argued that the spatial professional can't sit on an island anymore and should be a working part of a larger technical team. A second EarthStuff post pointed to a story about the Ontario "Lake America" change driving a surge in Google Maps alternatives.

*Why this matters:* The reinvention conversation has moved past panic and into job design. Pairing it with a story about people leaving a dominant platform for alternatives is a reminder that tool choice is now political as well as technical.
