**3. Local platforms fill the gap official mapping leaves in disaster response**

In Halle, Germany, a university and the city's climate team launched NASK, a public flood-memory platform where residents upload photos and reports from heavy rain events so the danger doesn't fade once the sun comes back out, geoObserver reports. In Nepal, Spatial Source covered an Australian aid mission flying drones with infrared, AI and 3D mapping to capture almost 200,000 square meters of flood aftermath terrain.

*Why this matters:* Landscape context notes that grassroots and demand-side voices rarely surface directly in the feeds. A university-city climate team and a fire-service aid mission publishing before any official geoportal does is exactly that gap closing, in real time, on two different continents.

## Top Five Posts

**1. The United States Is Replacing Every Official Latitude, Longitude And Height It Publishes, Existing Coordinates Will Move By As Much As Four Metres** — *EarthStuff*
EarthStuff surfaces NOAA's plan to retire NAD83 and NGVD88 and replace them with four new terrestrial reference frames and a new geopotential datum, shifting every published US coordinate by as much as four meters even though nothing on the ground actually moves. New submissions to the old system stop being accepted after January 13, 2027, and access shifts from occupying physical survey marks to satellite reference stations.
→ [Read it](https://earthstuff.substack.com/p/the-united-states-is-replacing-every)

**2. MovingPandas is moving to Codeberg** — *Free and Open Source GIS Ramblings*
Anita Graser is moving the widely-used MovingPandas trajectory analysis library off GitHub to Codeberg, citing a year of positive testing with Trajectools and a "further decline of GitHub performance." Codeberg's LLM usage policies come along with the move, adding a concrete data point to the industry's running argument over open data, AI training and who profits from the commons.
→ [Read it](https://anitagraser.com/2026/09/14/movingpandas-is-moving-to-codeberg/)

A guest post from geoinformatics researcher Detlef Thürkow walks through NASK, a platform built by the city of Halle and Martin Luther University where residents document flood damage with photos so the memory of a heavy rain event survives past the next sunny month. It layers citizen reports, fire department data and official DWD weather warnings onto one map.
