## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. The #30DayMapChallenge gets its themes**

Maps Mania says the 2026 #30DayMapChallenge themes are out and walks through the open-source web stack it plans to use, starting with MapLibre. #geoObserver passes along Topi Tjukanov's announcement too, with the challenge running every day of November from the 1st.

*Why this matters:* MapLibre and other web mapping frameworks get almost no technical coverage in these feeds. A community challenge is where that tooling actually gets exercised, so the stack choices people publish this month are worth reading as field reports.

**2. Hazard mapping with real places attached**

EarthStuff shared a paper on mapping invasive albizia trees against Hawai'i infrastructure, plus NOAA's Coastal Flood Exposure Mapper. Earth Observation News posted a burn chamber experiment using UAS to study fire and drought stress, a follow-up to fieldwork in Kruger National Park.

*Why this matters:* GeoAI coverage is mostly supply-side promises. These are named places, named hazards and decision-support framing for vegetation management and flood exposure. Conservation GIS is a chronic content gap, so the Kruger work is a welcome exception.

**3. Open data from the community side**

OpenStreetMap opened its 2026 Community Microgrants call, aimed at small efforts like mapping parties and editathons. Revolutionary GIS pointed to Moldova's 2024 census population datasets, published at commune, raion and locality level on the national statistics portal.

*Why this matters:* The open data debate has been about whether openness is enough. Here it's the plumbing that makes data usable, small grants for community effort and census layers released in granular, downloadable form. Quiet, but exactly the kind of work CNG's usefulness argument points at.
