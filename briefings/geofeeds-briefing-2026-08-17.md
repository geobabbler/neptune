## The Story That Stood Out

**Galileo Moves PNT Security From Hardware Add-On to Protocol Baseline**

Geoconnexion reported that Galileo's Signal Authentication Service (SAS) is now underway, giving civilian users ranging-level authentication on the E6-C signal component to complement the existing OSNMA message authentication. It lands three days after Earth Imaging Journal covered two hardware responses to the same jamming-and-spoofing problem — Javad GNSS's new GNSS+INS line and Infinite Electronics' ruggedized Pasternack antennas — and on the same day Geoconnexion also covered Fraunhofer IDMT's acoustic drone-detection system, which sidesteps GNSS and visual tracking entirely by localizing drones from sound alone. Read together, the direction is hard to miss: positioning trust is migrating out of the receiver and into the signal and sensor layer, rather than staying a hardware feature vendors sell as an upgrade.

*Why this matters:* Built-in signal authentication should worry vendors selling anti-spoofing hardware as a premium add-on — if the constellation verifies trust for free, that markup won't survive. The real story isn't Galileo's engineering; it's that GNSS resilience is becoming infrastructure everyone gets by default, not a product anyone competes to sell.
