Quiet day across the feeds — here are the highlights.

**Copernicus Browser made high-resolution wildfire tracking a default option.** ESA's Copernicus Browser rolled out a built-in "wildfires" visualization layer for Sentinel-2 imagery on August 4 — previously users had to paste in a custom script manually. Simon Proud, ESA's Sentinel-2 mission scientist, pushed to get it integrated after years of it circulating as a workaround; it renders active fires in white/yellow, burning vegetation in red, and burn scars in dark brown. At 10-meter resolution it's notably sharper than NASA's free FIRMS tool, which tops out around 250 meters.
→ [Europe has a free satellite service to track wildfires](https://medium.com/@sakaerka/europe-has-a-free-satellite-service-to-track-wildfires-2b635da2be4d?source=rss------earth_observation-5)

**Mappery relaunched its post map.** After more than a year offline following a plugin failure, Mappery rebuilt its clustering map from scratch, letting readers browse roughly 1,400 of the site's nearly 2,500 "Maps in the Wild" posts geographically, with pop-ups that tab through nearby entries.
→ [The Mappery Map is Back](https://mappery.org/the-mappery-map-is-back/)

→ [Do we really need big models in 2026?](https://brian.digitalmaddox.com/blog/?p=863)
