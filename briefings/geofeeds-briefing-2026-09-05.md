## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. The UN votes to retire Mercator, and independents react in real time**

The UN General Assembly voted Friday to formally phase out the Mercator projection in favor of the 2018 Equal Earth map, backing the long-running Correct the Map campaign's push to stop distorting Africa's true scale. The Map Room flagged the vote as it reached the UN floor, citing Guardian coverage of the campaign's arrival at the General Assembly. EarthStuff picked up the same Guardian story within hours, framing it as a UN endorsement of Africa's true size. Maps Mania went a step further and built an interactive slider comparing Equal Earth against Web Mercator so readers can drag between the two projections themselves.

*Why this matters:* Complaints about Mercator inflating the Global North have circulated in cartography circles for decades. A binding UN resolution turns a familiar argument about representation into an actual institutional decision, and gives independent cartographers a concrete event to build tools and commentary around instead of relitigating the same debate.

**2. Foundation models get skepticism while lidar lands on the Moon**

TerraWatch Space published a deep dive interrogating whether EO foundation models and the embeddings they produce live up to the hype, arguing the real economic value may end up with whoever holds proprietary data or owns the end user relationship rather than with the model builders themselves. The same day, AEye's long-range Apollo lidar was selected by Lunar Outpost for the Pegasus Lunar Terrain Vehicle that will carry Artemis astronauts across the Moon, described as opening a new market category in space mobility. One piece questions where AI value in EO will land, the other shows a physical sensor already winning a brand new market.

*Why this matters:* The Earth embeddings conversation has been quiet since April, and TerraWatch's skepticism fits that lull. A lidar sensor just won a genuinely new hardware market. Sensing hardware keeps landing concrete contracts while the AI software conversation stays stuck debating where the value will actually sit.

**3. Small acts of craft while the big story breaks**

Not every feed spent the day on the UN vote. Esri's Editor's Corner published a practical tutorial on using the multiple choice question type in ArcGIS Online forms. GeoHipster's monthly Maps and Mappers feature profiled Safiya Nijan, a GIS undergraduate at the University of Peradeniya, who turned GEBCO bathymetric data into a stylized 3D relief map of Lake Ontario using ArcGIS Pro and Photoshop. And The Map Room caught something stranger: a floating island that appeared on British Columbia's Williston Lake, visible in real satellite imagery rather than the centuries-old phantom-island errors it usually covers.

*Why this matters:* These are the posts nobody assigns. A vendor writing a form-building tip, a student turning bathymetric data into art, a blogger noticing a lake growing an island overnight. None of it moves markets, but it's the daily texture that keeps the ecosystem's tutorial and craft gaps from being total.
