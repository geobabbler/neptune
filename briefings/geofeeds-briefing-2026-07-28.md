## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. AI Is Redefining What "Geospatial Expertise" Means**

Ed Parsons argued that AI is shifting geographers away from mastering the "how" (shapefiles, projections, SQL) toward owning the "what" — the questions worth asking in the first place. Will Cadell's Strategic Geospatial post made a structurally similar case from a different angle: durable data outlasts the interfaces built on top of it, so organizations that treat data as disposable keep re-paying the same tax every time the UI layer changes.

*Why this matters:* Both pieces argue the industry's defensible value is migrating away from tooling fluency and toward data stewardship and framing the right problem — a more concrete, less panicked version of the "post-GIS" debate that dominated earlier in 2026.

**2. Geospatial Intelligence Is Moving From Demo to Deployed Operations**

Three separate posts show GEOINT operationalizing in the field rather than staying conceptual: ICEYE detailed embedding its space-based ISR into the Royal Netherlands Army's Fighter Lion exercise, the World Customs Organization used geospatial intelligence to catch smugglers along the Benin–Nigeria border in "Operation GEOCONTROL," and Project Geospatial's regional-hub series profiled Tampa's "Cyber Bay" as a live convergence point of defense intelligence and civilian infrastructure.

*Why this matters:* Government/defense remains the dominant EO customer segment in the feeds, but these posts show it moving past pilot-stage demos into tasking-to-intelligence cycles with measurable outcomes — a rare concrete counterpoint to the mostly aspirational GeoAI discourse elsewhere.

**3. GNSS Alternatives Are Getting Real Hardware, Not Just Roadmaps**

Spatial Source reported that Galileo satellites 33 and 34, launched in December, have now been commissioned into active service. The Map Room flagged Xona Space Systems' plans for a low-Earth-orbit navigation constellation designed to resist the interference and spoofing that plague traditional GPS.

*Why this matters:* Positioning resilience is quietly becoming its own infrastructure category — separate from the imagery/EO conversation — as both government constellations and commercial LEO entrants treat GNSS diversification as insurance against jamming and spoofing.
