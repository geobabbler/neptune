## Three Topics That Stood Out

**1. Governments keep deciding what the map says**

The Map Room reported that a law in effect since October 1 now requires Alabama state entities and employees to say "Gulf of America." The same blog noted Quebec still doesn't recognize its border with Labrador, which traces back to a 1927 Privy Council ruling. Revolutionary GIS flagged Texas's 2026 parcels on the state data portal, and FOSS4G NA published an open data and government agenda.

*Why this matters:* Names and boundaries are policy choices that end up baked into datasets. The Texas parcel release shows the other side, where public data gets more useful. The open data debate is shifting from "release everything" toward who defines the record and who maintains it.

**2. Hazard and ecosystem research aims at decision-makers**

EarthStuff shared a USACE fluvial flood exposure dataset covering the conterminous US, Alaska and Hawaii, the product of a two-year effort. Earth Observation News ran several posts from Germany's AK Fernerkundung meeting, including Bavarian grassland diversity mapping and ecosystem vulnerability to climate change. GoGeomatics reported Geo Week's keynote on bathymetric lidar of the Great Salt Lake.

*Why this matters:* Biodiversity and lidar are two of the ecosystem's chronic content gaps, and both show up here with real projects attached. Flood exposure data with national coverage is what insurers and planners actually need, even if they rarely write about it in these feeds.

**3. Energy and infrastructure buyers get the vendor treatment**

Geoconnexion covered the expanded PwC and Esri alliance aimed at energy and infrastructure organizations. The ArcGIS Blog promoted a Solar Panel Inspections solution for field teams, and Spatial Source reported Blue Marble's new three-tier Global Mapper subscription. Earth Imaging Journal carried Beam Global's agreement to acquire drone company ScoutDI for industrial and defense markets.

*Why this matters:* Commercial verticals are underrepresented here, and when they appear it's through the supply side. Vendors are bundling products, signing integrators and buying hardware. Buyers still aren't the ones writing. Watch whether subscription tiers and acquisitions become the norm for field data capture.
